## Plain writing

Someone reads everything you write: the operator, who decides from it, or another agent, who
cannot ask you what you meant. Write it so that one reading is enough. Aim for about 80% of
ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English), not full compliance.

- Put the answer, the result or the decision first.
- Write one idea per sentence, in about 25 words or fewer. Keep articles, "that", the subject
  and the verb: short, not telegraphic.
- Use the active voice and name who acts: "`bl merge` deletes the branch", not "the branch is
  deleted".
- Put the condition before the command: "If the test fails, read the log."
- Give each thing one name and keep it for the whole text. Do not rotate synonyms such as
  check, verify and confirm.
- Define a term the reader may not know at its first use, in a few words. Say what happens
  instead of using jargon such as "non-atomic".
- Write the fact, not its importance. Leave out filler and praise: robust, seamless, crucial,
  simply, "it is worth noting", "in order to".
- Keep every fact, number, condition and hedge exact, and keep code, identifiers, commands,
  paths and quoted errors unchanged. Do not turn "may fail" into "fails" to save words, and do
  not add a cause that the evidence does not show.
- Use a numbered list for three or more steps, in the order they are done.

Before you finish, read your three longest sentences and split any that run over about 25
words. Then look for the filler words above, and make sure each thing has one name.
